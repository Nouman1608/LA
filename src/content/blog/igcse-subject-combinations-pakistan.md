If your child has just started Grade 9, one of the biggest decisions of the next two years is already on the table: which IGCSE subjects to take. Get the combination right and A Level choices become straightforward. Get it wrong, and students often find themselves closing doors to Medicine, Engineering or Business before they've even reached Grade 11.

This guide walks Pakistani parents and students through building an IGCSE subject combination that actually supports the A Level and university path they want — using real Cambridge, Edexcel and AQA syllabus structures, not generic advice.

## Why IGCSE subject choice matters more than it seems

In the British curriculum system, IGCSE is not just "high school." Universities and A Level teachers look at IGCSE grades — especially in Maths and the Sciences — as evidence a student can handle the next level. More importantly, some A Level subjects are much harder to pick up in Grade 11 if the IGCSE foundation wasn't built first.

Three things Pakistani families consistently underestimate:

- **Board differences matter.** Cambridge, Edexcel and AQA don't offer identical subject lists. AQA, for instance, doesn't offer Accounting, Commerce or ICT — schools using AQA route those students through Combined Science or different electives instead.
- **Combined vs. separate sciences is a real fork in the road.** A student who does Combined Science at IGCSE and later wants A Level Biology, Chemistry and Physics separately will have gaps to fill.
- **"Keeping options open" has limits.** Ten subjects sounds safe, but it usually means less depth in the ones that actually matter for university admission.

## Start from the end goal, not the subject list

Before picking individual subjects, decide roughly which direction the student is leaning.

**Medicine, dentistry or pharmacy.** Universities want Biology, Chemistry and Physics as separate sciences — not Combined Science — plus strong Mathematics. Locking this in at IGCSE makes the move into A Level Biology, Chemistry and Physics far smoother.

**Engineering.** Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry are close to non-negotiable, with Additional Mathematics a strong asset where the school offers it. [Computer Science](/igcse/cambridge/computer-science/) is a useful fourth choice for software-adjacent engineering paths.

**Business, economics or finance.** Mathematics, Economics or Business Studies, and Accounting (where the board offers it) form a strong base. English and a science subject round out the combination — universities still like to see a science was studied, even for non-science applicants.

**Undecided.** Keep Mathematics, English and at least one science fixed, then choose three or four electives from Economics, Business Studies, Computer Science or a second science. This preserves the most A Level options later.

## Understand what each board actually offers

This is where a lot of subject-selection mistakes happen, because parents assume every board offers every subject.

**Cambridge (CAIE)** has the widest subject range and is the most common board in Pakistani private schools. It does not offer IGCSE Psychology or Law — those appear later, if at all, and only through certain boards.

**Edexcel** overlaps heavily with Cambridge on core subjects — Sciences, Maths, Business, Economics — but structures some papers differently, and it's the board most Pakistani schools use for A Level Urdu.

**AQA** is narrower — no Accounting, Commerce or ICT. Schools using AQA generally lean on it for Sciences, English and Maths, with Combined Science standing in as the IGCSE-equivalent where AQA has no separate-sciences IGCSE.

Ask your school directly which board each subject is examined under — it's common for one school to mix boards across subjects, using Cambridge for Sciences and Edexcel for Business Studies, for example.

## Combinations that work well

**Pre-Medical:** Biology, Chemistry, Physics (separate sciences), Mathematics, English, plus one elective — often Computer Science or Economics.

**Pre-Engineering:** Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Computer Science, English, plus one elective.

**Business or Commerce:** Mathematics, Economics, Business Studies, Accounting (Cambridge) or Computer Science, English.

**Keeping doors open:** Mathematics, English, and Biology, Chemistry, Physics as separate sciences. This single combination protects Medicine, Engineering and most Business or Economics routes simultaneously, at the cost of a heavier workload.

## Don't ignore workload and actual ability

A combination that looks impressive on paper but overwhelms the student usually backfires — IGCSE grades matter for IBCC equivalence and for A Level subject eligibility. It's generally better to score A/A* in seven well-chosen subjects than B/C in ten. If a student is genuinely struggling with a subject required for their target path — commonly Additional Mathematics or Physics — targeted 1-to-1 support at the IGCSE stage is far more effective than trying to fix the gap after moving to A Level.

## Frequently asked questions from Pakistani parents

**Can my child switch subjects after starting Grade 9?** It's possible in the first few months, but it gets harder — and often means catching up on missed foundational topics — once the school year is well underway. If a subject genuinely isn't working, raise it with the school early rather than waiting until IGCSE mocks.

**How many IGCSE subjects should a student actually take?** Most Pakistani private schools settle on seven to nine subjects. More isn't automatically better — universities and A Level admission are driven by grades in the subjects that matter for the chosen path, not by subject count.

**Does the exam board affect university admission later?** Not meaningfully. Universities recognise Cambridge, Edexcel and AQA as equivalent British curriculum qualifications. What matters is the grade achieved and, for STEM paths, whether Sciences were taken separately rather than as Combined Science.

**Should a student who wants Medicine take Combined Science instead of separate Sciences?** No — this is one of the most consequential choices in this whole guide. Combined Science covers less depth in each subject and can create real gaps when moving into A Level Biology, Chemistry and Physics. Separate sciences are the safer route for any Medicine-leaning student.

**When should subject choices actually be finalised?** Ideally before the Grade 9 school year begins, since IGCSE is typically a two-year programme. If your child has already started the term without full clarity, it's still worth revisiting now — a few weeks in is far better than after the first set of mock exams.
