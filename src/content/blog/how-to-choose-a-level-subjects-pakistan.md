IGCSE and O Level results arrive on 18 and 20 August 2026, and within weeks of that, thousands of Pakistani students face the first academic decision that genuinely shapes their future: which A Level subjects to take. Unlike O Levels, where the school largely sets your subjects, A Levels hand the choice to you — and the combination you pick can open or quietly close doors to medicine, engineering, business schools and universities abroad.

The good news: chosen deliberately, three subjects are enough to keep every path you care about open. This guide covers how to choose them — by career goal, by board, and by honest self-assessment — plus the mistakes we see students regret by the time university applications come around.

## Why this choice matters more than students expect

A Levels are specialist qualifications. Universities in Pakistan and abroad do not just look at your grades; they look at *which subjects* the grades are in. Medical colleges expect science combinations. Engineering programmes expect Mathematics and Physics. And if you plan to apply to Pakistani universities, the **IBCC equivalence** process converts your A Level results into local marks based partly on your subject combination — so a mismatch discovered in second year is expensive to fix. Check the current IBCC requirements for your intended field before finalising anything.

The right time to think about all of this is now, before the academic year starts — not after two months of a subject you picked by default.

## How many subjects should you take?

**Three A Levels is the standard, and for most students the right answer.** Universities in the UK and Pakistan build offers around three subjects; a fourth adds workload faster than it adds advantage. Strong students sometimes add a fourth where it serves a clear purpose — Further Mathematics for a competitive engineering or computer science application, for instance — but a fourth subject is never a substitute for top grades in three. Depth beats breadth at this level.

## Choosing by career path

### Medicine and dentistry

The non-negotiables are **Biology and Chemistry**, and nearly all aspiring medical students in Pakistan take **Physics** as the third subject to satisfy pre-medical equivalence and entry-test syllabi. If MBBS or BDS is even a possibility for you, build around [A Level Biology](/a-levels/cambridge/biology/) and Chemistry from day one — this is the combination that is hardest to retrofit later.

### Engineering and computer science

**Mathematics and Physics** form the core; the third subject is commonly Chemistry (kept for pre-engineering equivalence) or Computer Science for software-focused paths. Mathematics is the single most demanded subject across university programmes, and boards differ in how they structure it — Edexcel's modular [A Level Mathematics](/a-levels/edexcel/mathematics/), for example, is examined unit by unit, which some students find more forgiving than one linear set of finals.

### Business, economics, accounting and finance

A strong trio here is **Mathematics, Economics and Business or Accounting**. [A Level Economics](/a-levels/cambridge/economics/) teaches the analytical writing that business schools prize, Mathematics keeps quantitative degrees (and later, professional routes like ACCA and CFA) open, and Accounting or Business grounds the theory in practice.

### Law, social sciences and humanities

Law degrees in Pakistan and the UK rarely demand specific A Levels — they reward essay-writing and argument. English Language, History, Sociology, Psychology and Law itself are all sound choices; an essay-heavy combination signals exactly the skills admissions tutors want. Taking A Level Law is not required to study law at university, but it is an honest preview of whether the field suits you.

### Still undecided?

Pick subjects that keep doors open rather than close them. Mathematics plus two subjects you are demonstrably good at is the classic flexible combination — it satisfies more university entry requirements than any other single subject and pairs with everything from economics to computer science.

## Cambridge, Edexcel or AQA?

Most Pakistani schools teach Cambridge (CAIE), and for school candidates the board is usually decided for you. Private candidates and students switching after O Levels have a real choice, and the structural differences matter. **Cambridge** A Levels can be taken staged (AS first, then A2) or as one final sitting, with June and November exam series available in Pakistan. **Edexcel** International A Levels are fully modular — units can be sat and retaken individually across January, June and October/November sessions, which spreads pressure and makes grade recovery easier. **AQA** (through OxfordAQA internationally) offers linear qualifications with a syllabus style many find clean and predictable. All three are accepted identically by universities and by IBCC; choose based on exam structure and where you can realistically get good teaching, not on prestige rumours.

## Five mistakes to avoid

**Choosing what your friends chose.** Your friends are not sitting your entry test or writing your personal statement. Combinations chosen socially are the most commonly regretted.

**Ignoring your O Level evidence.** Your O Level or IGCSE grades are real data. A student who fought for a C in O Level Chemistry will find A Level Chemistry a heavy lift — the jump in depth between the two levels is significant in every science.

**Taking all-new subjects at once.** One unfamiliar subject is an experiment; three is a gamble. Anchor your combination in subjects where you have proven footing.

**Overloading to look impressive.** Four or five weak A Levels impress no one. Admissions decisions run on grades in three.

**Leaving the decision to the last week.** Classes begin in late August and September, and the first term sets the tone for the whole qualification. Students who start behind spend a year catching up.

## A sensible timeline from results day

Results arrive 18–20 August 2026. Spend the following week matching possible combinations against the university programmes you are considering — check actual entry requirements, not hearsay. Aim to finalise subjects by the end of August, and start classes in September with the decision behind you. If you are torn between two subjects, a trial class in each will tell you more than another week of deliberation.
