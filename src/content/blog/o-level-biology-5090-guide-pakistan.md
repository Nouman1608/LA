Cambridge O Level Biology (5090) is one of the most-taken science electives in Pakistan, largely because it's the natural first step toward Medicine, Dentistry, and A Level Biology. It's also a subject a lot of students underestimate — the syllabus is large, and much of the exam rewards precise scientific communication rather than just knowing the facts. This guide covers the syllabus, exam structure, and a realistic preparation plan for the current 2026–2028 syllabus.

## What Is O Level Biology (5090)?

Biology 5090 is a Cambridge O Level science subject that builds understanding of living organisms, biological systems, and the processes that sustain life — from cell structure and enzymes to human physiology, genetics, and ecosystems. For Pakistani students, it usually sits alongside Chemistry and Physics as one of three sciences taken toward a pre-medical or pre-engineering pathway, though it's also taken as a standalone science option.

The current syllabus (version 3, for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028) applies to students sitting exams in this window, so if you're studying from an older edition of your textbook, it's worth double-checking your notes against the latest syllabus document, particularly the command word list, which was updated in this version.

## Syllabus at a Glance

The 5090 syllabus is organised into topics that build on each other rather than standing alone, which is why gaps early in the syllabus tend to cause problems later. The main areas are:

1. Characteristics and classification of living organisms
2. Organisation of the organism (cells, tissues, organs)
3. Movement into and out of cells (diffusion, osmosis, active transport)
4. Biological molecules (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, enzymes)
5. Enzymes and factors affecting their activity
6. Plant nutrition (photosynthesis, mineral nutrition)
7. Human nutrition (digestion, balanced diet)
8. Transport in plants and animals (circulatory systems, xylem, phloem)
9. Respiration (aerobic and anaerobic)
10. Excretion, coordination and response (nervous system, sense organs)
11. Reproduction (asexual and sexual, in plants and humans)
12. Organisms and their environment (food chains, ecosystems)
13. Human influences on ecosystems (pollution, conservation)
14. Biotechnology and genetic engineering

Topics 3–5 (cell transport, biological molecules, and enzymes) come up constantly across the whole syllabus — enzyme activity, in particular, resurfaces in digestion, respiration, and photosynthesis questions — so it's worth over-preparing this section rather than treating it as one isolated topic.

## Exam Structure: Papers, Marks and Timing

All candidates take Paper 1 and Paper 2. For the practical component, centres choose between Paper 3 (a hands-on practical test) or Paper 4 (Alternative to Practical, a written paper testing the same skills without lab access). Most private candidates and online students in Pakistan sit Paper 4, since it doesn't require a school laboratory.

| Paper | Type | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 1 hour | 40 | ~27% |
| Paper 2 | Theory | 1 hour 45 minutes | 80 | ~53% |
| Paper 3 | Practical Test | 1 hour 30 minutes | — | ~20% |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Practical | 1 hour | 40 | ~20% |

Paper 2 carries the largest share of marks and mixes short-answer, structured, and extended-response questions, so command words (*describe*, *explain*, *compare*, *suggest*) matter enormously here — answering a "describe" question with an "explain" answer, or vice versa, is one of the most common reasons students lose marks despite knowing the content.

Paper 4 (Alternative to Practical) tests experimental and data-handling skills through written questions rather than a live experiment — expect questions on food tests (Benedict's, iodine, Biuret, ethanol emulsion test), enzyme and temperature investigations, osmosis in plant tissue, and interpreting graphs or tables from experimental scenarios.

## How to Prepare for an A* in Biology 5090

**Understand before you memorise.** Biology rewards students who can explain *why* a process happens, not just recite it — an examiner can tell the difference between a memorised sentence and genuine understanding, and marks are awarded accordingly.

**Treat diagrams as a scoring opportunity, not an afterthought.** Diagrams appear throughout Paper 2 and carry real marks. Practise drawing the heart, leaf cross-section, and cell structures neatly, in pencil, with labels written horizontally — messy or ambiguous labelling loses marks even when the diagram itself is correct.

**Learn command words properly.** *State* wants a short, precise fact. *Describe* wants what happens, in sequence, without reasons. *Explain* wants the reasoning or mechanism behind it. *Suggest* invites you to apply your knowledge to an unfamiliar scenario. Misreading the command word is one of the most avoidable ways to drop marks.

**Make past papers your core revision tool, not a final step.** Past papers reveal exactly which topics recur, how marks are distributed within a question, and where most students lose marks according to examiner reports. We cover a full system for this in [How to Use Past Papers to Raise Your Grade in 2026](/blog/how-to-use-past-papers-effectively/) — the same approach applies directly to Biology 5090's Paper 1, 2 and 4.

**Build a weekly revision rhythm early.** Biology's syllabus is large enough that cramming genuinely doesn't work — spaced, weekly revision of earlier topics (alongside new ones) is what keeps enzyme kinetics fresh by the time you reach genetics three months later.

## Common Mistakes Students Make

- Learning definitions word-for-word without understanding the underlying process, which falls apart the moment a question rephrases it.
- Underestimating Paper 4 by assuming "alternative to practical" means easier — it tests real skills and is frequently under-revised.
- Ignoring examiner reports, which spell out — often in plain language — exactly where past candidates lost marks on each topic.
- Leaving diagram practice until the week before the exam.
