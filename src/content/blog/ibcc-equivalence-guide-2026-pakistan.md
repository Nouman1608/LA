Cambridge released A Level results on 11 August, with IGCSE and O Level results following on 18 August 2026 (Pearson Edexcel's International A Level and IGCSE results land on 13 and 20 August). For most Pakistani students, results day isn't the finish line — it's the start of a paperwork race. If you're applying to a Pakistani university, medical college, engineering programme, or government scholarship, you'll need an IBCC equivalence certificate before you can be considered, and the process takes weeks you may not have to spare.

Here's exactly what IBCC equivalence is, how it works for O Level and A Level results, and how to avoid the mistakes that push families past admission deadlines.

## What Is IBCC Equivalence, and Why Do You Need It?

The Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC) is the federal body that converts international qualifications — Cambridge, Edexcel, AQA — into the local Matric and Intermediate (FA/FSc) equivalent. Pakistani universities, medical and engineering colleges, HEC-recognised institutions, and most government scholarship and job applications require this certificate — not your original Cambridge or Edexcel statement of results — to assess your merit position.

In short: without an equivalence certificate, most local admission offices in Pakistan have no official way to place your O Level or A Level grades on their merit list.

## O Level Equivalence: Your Matric Equivalent

O Level results are treated as equivalent to the Secondary School Certificate (SSC/Matric). IBCC generally expects a minimum number of subjects — including compulsory areas — passed at a satisfactory grade, alongside your elective subjects such as sciences, business, or humanities. If your O Levels covered a broad science group — for example [O Level Chemistry](/o-levels/cambridge/chemistry/) alongside Biology, Physics and Mathematics — that combination typically maps cleanly onto the science-group SSC equivalence.

Because exact subject-count and grade thresholds are reviewed periodically, always confirm the current requirement on IBCC's official portal or with your admission office before you assume you qualify — don't rely on last year's rules.

## A Level Equivalence: Your FA/FSc Equivalent

A Level results convert to the Intermediate (FA/FSc) equivalent — but there's a sequencing rule that catches a lot of families out: **IBCC will not process an A Level equivalence application until your O Level (SSC) equivalence certificate has already been issued.** If you haven't applied for O Level equivalence yet, that has to happen first, even if you sat your O Levels years ago.

Your subject group matters here too. Medical programme applicants typically need a pre-medical-style combination — Biology, Chemistry and often Physics, such as students who took [A Level Biology](/a-levels/cambridge/biology/) alongside Chemistry. Engineering applicants generally need a pre-engineering combination built around Physics, Chemistry and [A Level Mathematics](/a-levels/cambridge/mathematics/). Commerce and humanities pathways have their own accepted groupings. If your subject combination doesn't obviously map onto one of these local groups, confirm with IBCC or your target university before you apply, rather than after.

## Documents You'll Need

Requirements can shift slightly year to year, but families should generally be ready with:

- Your original statement of results or certificate for each qualification
- School leaving or transfer certificate
- CNIC, B-Form, or NADRA smart card number
- Recent passport-size photographs
- Paid fee voucher for the equivalence application
- Your O Level equivalence certificate, if you're applying for A Level equivalence

One detail worth knowing early: a **provisional statement of results is typically valid for only around six months.** Wait too long to apply, and you may be asked for your original printed certificate instead — which arrives from Cambridge or Edexcel later than your statement of results. That's one more reason not to delay.

## How to Apply: The Process

1. **Register on the IBCC online equivalence portal** and create your application.
2. **Upload your documents** — clear, complete scans matter; blurry or partial uploads are one of the most common causes of delay.
3. **Pay the processing fee** through the specified channel.
4. **Wait for verification.** IBCC checks your documents against your qualification and subject group.
5. **Receive your equivalence certificate**, generally within a few weeks of a complete, correctly submitted application — though timelines vary by season and application volume, so treat this as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Results season (August and September) is IBCC's busiest period, since thousands of O Level and A Level students all apply within the same few weeks. Applying early in that window, rather than waiting until an admission deadline is days away, gives you a real buffer.

## Common Mistakes That Delay a Certificate

- **Name mismatches.** Your name on your CNIC/B-Form, school records, and Cambridge/Edexcel certificate needs to match exactly. Even small spelling differences can trigger a manual review and add weeks.
- **Applying for A Level equivalence before O Level equivalence is finalised.** As above, this simply won't be processed.
- **Letting the provisional statement expire** before applying, then scrambling for the original certificate.
- **Incomplete subject documentation** — a missing results slip for one sitting, or a submission for the wrong exam series.
- **Assuming last year's subject-group or grade requirements still apply.** Confirm current rules before you submit, not after a rejection.

## Timing It Right This Results Season

If you're one of the thousands of Cambridge or Edexcel students collecting results across 11–20 August 2026, the practical move is to gather your documents this week — rather than after you've celebrated, or after you've spent days processing a disappointing grade. Universities and medical/engineering colleges publish their own admission and merit-list deadlines soon after results, often within a few weeks, and a delayed equivalence certificate can knock you out of a merit list through no fault of your academic performance.

If your results also have you weighing a retake in the October/November session — to strengthen a grade your equivalence percentage depends on, or to meet a specific subject requirement — that's a separate, and very time-sensitive, decision. Either way, getting your paperwork moving now keeps every option open.
