Results season is here. Cambridge releases AS and A Level results for the May/June 2026 series on **Tuesday 11 August 2026**, with IGCSE and O Level results following on **Tuesday 18 August 2026** — both at 05:00 GMT, which is 10:00 am in Pakistan. For thousands of Pakistani students, the next two weeks decide what happens next: university applications, AS to A2 planning, or a retake in the October/November session.

Whether you are expecting straight A*s or bracing yourself, the worst position to be in is not knowing your options. This guide walks through exactly what to do after results day — including the remark and retake deadlines that matter for students in Pakistan, some of which arrive sooner than you might think.

## Key dates at a glance

- **11 August 2026** — Cambridge AS & A Level results released (11:00 am Pakistan time)
- **13 August 2026** — Pearson Edexcel International A Level results released
- **18 August 2026** — Cambridge IGCSE & O Level results released (11:00 am Pakistan time)
- **20 August 2026** — Pearson Edexcel International GCSE results released
- **3 September 2026** — Late-stage and retake entry deadline for Cambridge October/November 2026 exams in Pakistan (British Council)
- **17 September 2026** — Late-stage entry deadline for Edexcel October/November 2026 exams in Pakistan
- **18 September 2026** — Deadline for Cambridge enquiries about results (remarks) for the June 2026 series

One date needs special attention: the **normal-stage deadline for Cambridge October/November entries in Pakistan is 6 August 2026 — before results even come out**. If you already know you will be sitting a paper in November, enter now at normal fees. Everyone else who decides to retake after seeing their grades will use the late/retake stage, which closes on 3 September.

## How to check your results

If you sat your exams through a school, your school receives results on release day and passes them to you — many schools now share statements of results online or over email the same morning. If you sat as a private candidate through the British Council, you will receive your statement of results from the British Council; keep an eye on the email address you registered with.

Have your candidate number and centre number ready either way. Your **statement of results** shows your grade per subject; the printed certificates arrive some weeks later.

## If your results are what you hoped for

Congratulations — take the win, then plan the next stage while momentum is on your side.

AS students moving into A2 should map out the year early: the A2 syllabus is a step up in depth, and the students who start in August rather than October consistently find the spring exam season calmer. If you finished [O Level](/o-levels/cambridge/physics/) or IGCSE, your next decision is choosing A Level subjects — a choice worth making deliberately, because university pathways in Pakistan and abroad depend on your combination.

## If you think a grade is wrong: enquiries about results

A disappointing grade is not necessarily the final word. Cambridge runs an **enquiries about results (EAR)** service for exactly this situation. There are two main options.

### Clerical recheck

An administrative check: were all pages marked, and were the marks added up and recorded correctly? No re-marking takes place.

### Review of marking

A senior examiner reviews your script to check the mark scheme was applied correctly. This is the option most students mean when they say "remark."

Three things to know before you apply. First, enquiries go through your exam centre — your school, or the British Council if you were a private candidate — not directly to Cambridge. Second, the **deadline for the June 2026 series is 18 September 2026**, and centres usually set internal cut-offs a few days earlier, so start the process the week you get results. Third, on a review of marking your mark can go up, stay the same, or go down — so a remark makes the most sense when you are a small number of marks from a boundary and your own working (or your teacher's judgement) suggests the grade undersells you.

## Retaking in October/November 2026: the Pakistan deadlines

Cambridge runs two exam series a year that Pakistani students can sit — May/June and October/November — so a retake does not mean losing a whole year. It usually means a focused ten-to-twelve-week push.

For private candidates in Pakistan, entries go through the **British Council's School Registration System (SRS)**:

- **Cambridge:** normal stage closed 6 August 2026; **late stage and retake entries close Thursday 3 September 2026**
- **Edexcel:** normal stage closes 21 August 2026; late stage closes 17 September 2026

Registration only counts once **payment is complete** — an unpaid entry misses the deadline. You will need a CNIC, B-form or NADRA smart card number to register, and fees at the late/retake stage can differ from normal-stage fees, so confirm the current amounts on the British Council SRS when you enter.

Two board-specific points worth knowing. Cambridge A Levels in the staged route let you re-sit AS or carry forward your AS mark, depending on your situation — check the carry-forward rules before entering. Edexcel International A Levels are modular, so you can retake an individual unit in October/November 2026 or in the January 2027 series without redoing the whole qualification.

### Should you actually retake?

A retake is worth it when the grade blocks something specific — a university offer, a merit list, an equivalence requirement — and when the gap is realistically closable. A student one grade short in [A Level Chemistry](/a-levels/cambridge/chemistry/) who lost marks on paper technique is a strong retake candidate. A student who was underprepared across the syllabus needs a more honest conversation about timelines and support. It is also fine *not* to retake: if your grades meet the requirements for your chosen path, a B on the certificate and a place on the course beats six more months of pressure.

## Make the retake count: study differently this time

The November session rewards precision. There is no time to re-read the whole textbook, so the sequence that works is: diagnose exactly which papers and topics cost you marks (your statement of results and, where available, component marks help here), rebuild those specific areas, then drill past papers under timed conditions against the mark scheme. Subjects like [A Level Mathematics](/a-levels/cambridge/mathematics/) respond especially well to this — most dropped marks trace to a handful of question types that targeted practice can fix.
