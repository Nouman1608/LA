Results day is stressful enough without also being several time zones away from the exam board's home country and, often, from extended family who've been through it before. If you're a Pakistani expat family in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh or Jeddah, here's a straightforward checklist for the days before and after results are released.

## Before results day

* Confirm how your child will receive results — through the school portal, CAIE Direct, or the school office — and check the exact release time in your local time zone, not Pakistan's or the UK's.
* Note the deadlines for remarks (Enquiries About Results) and retakes early. These windows are short and close quickly once results are out.
* If your child is applying to university in Pakistan, start gathering what IBCC equivalence will need alongside the results — it's one less thing to do under time pressure.

## On the day

* Read the grade against the school's own predicted grade and grade boundaries for that exam series, not last year's boundaries — they shift.
* If a subject is close to a grade boundary, a remark is usually worth requesting before deciding on a retake — it's cheaper and faster.
* If a retake is the right call, check the next available exam series for your board (Cambridge, Edexcel or AQA) and register within the window your school or exam centre sets. Our [private candidate registration guide](/blog/private-candidate-registration-pakistan-oct-nov-2026/) covers the Pakistan-side deadlines if you're registering through the British Council.

## In the following weeks

* Start the IBCC equivalence process early if university applications in Pakistan are on the table — processing times are longer than most families expect.
* For Gulf-based students applying to universities in the UAE, Saudi Arabia or further afield, check whether the institution needs an equivalence certificate or accepts the Cambridge/Edexcel certificate directly.
* If a subject grade will affect A Level subject choices or university offers, get that conversation started with the school as soon as possible — waiting rarely helps.
