Oct/Nov 2026 exams are under way or about to start for many students, but those aiming for the May/June 2027 series should already be planning. This guide explains what is known, what is not yet confirmed, and how to use the next six months well.

## What is confirmed and what is not

Cambridge has not publicly released the May/June 2027 timetable. A provisional timetable exists only on Cambridge's school-facing portal. Based on past series, the usual pattern is:

- **Exam window:** roughly late April to early June. For reference, the May/June 2026 series ran 23 April to 9 June 2026.
- **Final entries deadline:** around February 2027 for Cambridge's international deadline (an estimate, not an official date). Private candidates in Pakistan register through the British Council, whose deadlines are earlier than the international ones, so expect a date before this and confirm it with them as soon as registration opens.
- **Late entries:** around mid-April 2027 (an estimate; late fees normally apply).
- **Results:** around mid-August. For reference, May/June 2026 results were released on 18–19 August 2026.

Please treat all 2027 dates as provisional until your school or the British Council confirms them. Edexcel runs its own calendar and publishes its own dates, so Edexcel candidates should check Pearson's official information separately.

## School candidate or private candidate?

How you register depends on your situation. School candidates are entered by their school's exams officer, so ask your school for their internal deadline, which is usually earlier than Cambridge's. Private candidates must register through an authorised centre in Pakistan, such as the British Council, and must book their own exam slots. Check the centre's current fees and deadlines directly, because they change each year.

## A realistic six-month plan

If your exam is in May/June, you have roughly six months from now. A simple structure:

**Now to December:** finish the syllabus. Any topic not yet taught should be covered by the end of the year, so that January onward is for practice, not first-time learning.

**January to February:** complete topic-by-topic past paper questions. Use the Oct/Nov 2026 results (due in January 2027) as a signal: if you sat a retake, adjust your plan accordingly.

**March to April:** full timed papers, marked against official mark schemes. If you are weighing a March series, ask your centre whether it is offered to you in Pakistan.

**Final weeks:** error-log review, formula and definition recall, and exam-day logistics.

For method, see [how to use past papers to raise your grade](/blog/how-to-use-past-papers-effectively/).

## What to ask your school or centre this month

1. What is the internal and final entries deadline?
2. Which syllabus codes will I be entered for? (Check these carefully; a wrong code is hard to fix later.)
3. Which exam centre will I sit at, and what is the fee?
4. Do any of my subjects have a practical or coursework component with its own deadline?

## Mistakes that cost students marks (and money)

**Waiting for the timetable before planning.** The official timetable usually arrives only a few months before the series. Your preparation cannot wait for it; the syllabus and past papers are already available.

**Assuming every subject runs the same way.** Some subjects have separate papers, practical components or options. Ask your teacher for the syllabus PDF for each subject and read the assessment section, so you know exactly how many papers you will sit and how long each is.

**Registering late.** Late entries normally carry extra fees, and seats at exam centres can fill up. If you are a private candidate, start the process as soon as registration opens rather than at the deadline.

**Leaving weak topics until the end.** Be honest about your weakest two or three topics in each subject and book time for them now. A topic you fix in November takes a few hours; the same topic in April takes a panic.

**Ignoring clashes.** When timetables are released, check for papers on the same day or consecutive days with heavy workload, and adjust your revision order accordingly. Cambridge arranges clash resolution through your centre, so tell them early.

## A quick note for parents

Your child's school or centre handles the formal entry, but you can help by keeping a single calendar for deadlines, fee payments and exam dates, and by confirming the exact syllabus codes in writing. Also keep your child's Oct/Nov 2026 results date (January 2027) in mind: if a retake is under consideration, the May/June entry deadline will arrive soon after results.
